Essential Things to Prepare for When Traveling to Roatán

August 20, 2026 Roatan Island Style Tours 6 Min Read

Planning a trip to Roatán is exciting, but island life comes with its own unique set of quirks. From handling cash to dealing with sand flies, knowing how things actually work here will make the difference between a smooth vacation and a stressful one.

1 Cash vs. Credit Cards & US Dollars

While larger hotels and tourist restaurants accept major credit cards, cash is king across smaller shops, beach bars, water taxis, and local food stands. US Dollars (USD) are widely accepted everywhere alongside Honduran Lempiras (HNL).

However, local vendors and banks are extremely strict about US currency condition. Bills must be crisp and completely free of tears, ink marks, or heavy wear. Bring plenty of small denominations ($1, $5, $10, $20), as getting change for a $50 or $100 bill in smaller spots can be difficult. Also, keep in mind that ATMs are scarce on the East End, so withdraw or exchange cash before heading past French Harbour.

Local Advice: Check your USD bills before leaving home. Any bill with even a tiny rip or pen mark will likely be rejected by island merchants.

2 Sand Flies & Reef-Safe Protection

Roatán’s beaches are world-class, but like many tropical islands, sand flies (often called no-see-ums) inhabit the beaches, particularly during calm mornings, late afternoons, or overcast days. Standard OFF or DEET bug sprays often don't keep them away.

Instead, use oil-based repellents, baby oil, or locally made coconut oil sprays—these create a light physical barrier that prevents them from landing. When swimming on our delicate Mesoamerican Reef, always wear **mineral/zinc reef-safe sunscreen** to protect coral health.

3 Getting Around: Independent Exploration vs. Guided Tours

If you plan to explore the island on your own or in a small group, understanding local transport options is essential. Roatán has regular road taxis, water taxis in places like Oak Ridge and Jonesville, and rental cars available near the airport.

Always establish and agree on the total ride fare with your driver or captain **before stepping inside any taxi or boat**. Standard road taxis operate on a per-person or negotiated route basis, not meters. If you want a stress-free day exploring the island without constantly haggling or navigating unfamiliar roads, booking a private island tour with a local guide is often the best choice.

Transit Tip: Water taxis are the main way to travel between overwater spots in Oak Ridge and Jonesville. Always confirm whether the fare is per person or for the whole group before casting off.

4 Island Logistics & Utilities

Roatán operates on standard US electrical outlets (110V, 60Hz), so travellers from North America won't need plug adapters. Tap water on the island is generally not recommended for drinking; always stick to bottled or purified water, which is readily available everywhere.

Embrace "island time"—service at restaurants and local shops is relaxed and laid-back. Sit back, enjoy the breeze, and don't expect fast-food urgency when ordering fresh seafood made to order!

5 Essential Packing Gear for Island Excursions

Beyond swimwear and shorts, a few specific items will greatly improve your island outings:

• Water Shoes: Crucial for entering the water near ironshore coral or rocky shorelines.
• Dry Bag: Keeps your phone, camera, and cash dry during mangrove boat rides and water taxi trips.
• UV Rashguard: Protects your skin during long snorkeling sessions without relying heavily on sunscreen.
